What you pay at your border, and what decides it.
## The three things that set your bill
1. **Declared value.** What you actually paid. We do not under-declare or mark shipments as gifts.
2. **The tariff code.** Every item is classified under an HS code, and that code carries a duty rate.
3. **Your country's threshold.** Most countries let low-value shipments in duty free up to a limit.
## FOB and CIF
Countries calculate duty on one of two bases:
- **FOB** - goods value plus costs up to Incheon Airport.
- **CIF** - FOB plus insurance and international shipping.
On a CIF country the shipping cost is taxed too, so a cheap item with expensive express shipping can be charged more than you expect. Your country page says which applies.
## How you pay: DDP, DAP, DDU
- **DDP** - Delivered Duty Paid. You pay estimated duty and tax to us at checkout. Nothing to pay on arrival. Any overpayment of more than ₩5,000 is refunded as Store Credit.
- **DAP (Delivered At Place):** Import duties, taxes, and carrier fees are billed upon arrival. Refusing delivery does not cancel these charges. Refused shipments may be returned, stored, or disposed of by the carrier or customs, and all related costs remain the customer's responsibility.
- **DDU** - Delivered Duty Unpaid. The old name for DAP. It was retired from Incoterms, so if you see it elsewhere it means the same thing: you pay on arrival.
Some services are locked to one method. FedEx International Connect Plus is DDP only. FedEx International Priority, EMS Korea Post, and SF Express are DAP only. International Standard Shipping supports both, depending on destination.
Customs inspections, clearance delays, storage at the border, and document requests are outside our control and can add cost or time under either method.
## Import charges
Import charges cover duties, tariffs, import taxes, VAT and GST, customs handling fees, and brokerage fees. Duties and government taxes are set by your country. Customs handling fees are charged by the carrier or by us.

## Worked examples
Illustrative only. Your country page has the real figures for your destination. Our service fees and Korean domestic shipping normally form part of the FOB or CIF value, but they are left out of these examples to keep the arithmetic clear.
**Under the threshold**
- Goods \$80, shipping \$25, threshold \$150 CIF
- CIF value: \$105
- Duty: none
- VAT may still apply depending on the country
**Over the threshold, FOB country**
- Goods \$200, shipping \$40
- Duty base: \$200, because shipping is excluded
- Duty at 10%: \$20, plus VAT on the applicable base
**Over the threshold, CIF country**
- Same order, but shipping counts
- Duty base: \$240
- Duty at 10%: \$24
- The same goods cost \$4 more in duty purely because of the shipping method
**DDP prepaid** Estimated duty and tax \$50, plus the DDP customs handling fee for your service. If customs actually assesses \$45, the \$5 difference in duty is refunded as Store Credit where it exceeds ₩5,000. The handling fee is not refunded.
## If prepaid duty falls short
We cover the difference in most cases. We do not cover it where:
- you misdeclared the value or contents
- documents or information provided by you were incorrect, incomplete, or misleading
- customs rules changed after the shipment left Korea but before it cleared your border
- additional charges arise after shipment, or the original estimate was based on incorrect, incomplete, or changed information
## Who clears your box
We prepare export documents and hand your box to the carrier. Clearance is done by the carrier, a licensed customs broker, and your country's customs authority. We are not a licensed customs broker and cannot act as one.
If customs revalues or reclassifies your shipment, the resulting charge is yours.
## Customs and Tax ID numbers
Several countries will not release a parcel without a personal tax or customs ID for the recipient. If yours needs one, it is listed on your country page.
